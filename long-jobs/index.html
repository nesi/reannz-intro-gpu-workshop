    B3: Long jobs: time limits, checkpoints and the queue¶

    Beyond the basics

    Not covered in the live workshop. Read it before you queue a GPU job that will run for more than a day.

    Lesson Objectives

    • Choose a time limit from a measurement, not a guess
    • Know why a long GPU job should checkpoint, and what to save
    • Read why a job is pending, and know what makes one start sooner

    Questions

    • How long should I ask for?
    • What happens when a job reaches its time limit?
    • My job has been pending for hours. Why?

    How long to ask for¶

    Putting it all together set --time from the test job's step count: its rate, times the steps the real run needs, plus 20–30%. That margin matters, because of what happens at the limit:

    • Slurm stops the job when its time runs out, finished or not. seff reports it as TIMEOUT.
    • Everything since the job last saved its state is lost. For a three-day job that saves nothing until the end, that is three GPU-days.
    • No single job can run for longer than 21 days.

    Asking for far too long is not free either. A longer job is harder for the scheduler to fit in, so it waits longer to start (more on that below).

    Checkpointing¶

    A checkpoint is the job saving enough of its state to carry on from that point later. The cluster's advice is to checkpoint any job that runs longer than a day (Job checkpointing).

    What to save is everything needed to carry on, and nothing else:

    Kind of work What a checkpoint holds
    Training a model The model's weights, the optimiser's state, and the step count
    A simulation The program's own restart files. Most simulation codes write them, and can be told to start from one

    How often is a trade: often enough that losing the time since the last one would not hurt, and rarely enough that writing it does not slow the job down. Every half hour to every few hours suits most jobs.

    For PyTorch, the pattern is to save as you go, and to start from the last save if there is one:

    import os
import torch

start = 0
if os.path.exists("checkpoint.pt"):
    saved = torch.load("checkpoint.pt")
    model.load_state_dict(saved["model"])
    optimiser.load_state_dict(saved["optimiser"])
    start = saved["step"] + 1

for step in range(start, total_steps):
    train_one_step(model, optimiser)
    if step % 1000 == 0:
        torch.save({"model": model.state_dict(),
                    "optimiser": optimiser.state_dict(),
                    "step": step}, "checkpoint.pt")

    Run it as a chain of shorter jobs¶

    Once a job can pick up where it left off, it no longer has to be one long job. Split it into a chain, each link starting when the last one finishes:

    sbatch train.sl                                 # Submitted batch job 1234567
sbatch --dependency=afterok:1234567 train.sl    # starts when that one succeeds

    Shorter jobs start sooner, a failure costs you one link rather than the whole run, and a chain can run for longer than 21 days.

    Why is my job pending?¶

    squeue --me gives the reason in its last column:

    Reason Means What to do
    Priority Other jobs are ahead of yours Wait. A smaller, shorter request starts sooner
    Resources Your job is next, and is waiting for the cards, cores or memory it asked for to come free Wait, or ask for a card there are more of
    QOSMaxGRESPerUser You already hold as many GPUs as one person may at a time Wait for one of your own jobs to finish
    Dependency It is waiting for another job, as above Nothing; that is what you asked for

    squeue --me --start shows when Slurm expects a pending job to start.

    Each user may hold 6 GPUs at once, and 14 GPU-days' worth of running jobs — whichever comes first. The Job limits page has the full list.

    What makes a GPU job start sooner¶

    • The smallest card that fits. There are more of them, and fewer people want them (Which GPU: will it fit?).
    • An honest --time. The scheduler fills gaps in the queue with jobs short enough to finish before the gap closes. A job that asks for a week cannot use a gap of a day.
    • Only what you use. Spare cores and memory make a job harder to place, for no benefit.

    Keypoints

    • Set --time from the test job's step count, plus 20–30%. At the limit the job is stopped, finished or not.
    • Checkpoint any job longer than a day: save the state, and start from the last save if there is one.
    • A checkpointed job can run as a chain of shorter jobs, with --dependency=afterok.
    • squeue --me gives the reason a job is waiting. Smaller, shorter, honest requests start sooner.
